# Diagnose an upper-body defect on a new scan

English · [简体中文](chest-and-arms.zh-CN.md)

Open the delivered bare GLB and the original scan at the same scale and pose. Reproduce the reported angle, then orbit the shoulder, nape and underside of the jaw. In Blender, isolate the body, head, hair and garments one at a time. If surfaces share an object, isolate their faces in Edit Mode. Compare textured and neutral-material views. A defect visible in neutral material needs a geometry, normal or binding check. A band visible only with materials needs an atlas and shading check. Compare rest with the pose that exposes the defect.

## Start from the intact body fit

For a covered torso, follow the production entry point in [scan-transfer.py](../../../scripts/scan-transfer.py) and its [surface fitter](../../../scripts/transfer/body.py). It welds coincident positions, projects the whole body into the registered cameras and solves the displacement against the template surface with Laplacian regularization. Use the current character's prior, guide and working frame. For a repair, identify the last intact bare export. Read the current transfer report’s `stage2_body_fit` to identify the contour file and registered front and profile views used by the fitter. Compare those inputs with the exported skin at matching anatomical stations. If a compatible reference build is supplied, trace its export to its inputs before reusing the fitting procedure.

Before changing the arms, identify the sternum, left and right pectoral prominence, lower pectoral border, anterior axillary folds and lateral ribs. Record front positions and profile depths in the rib cage frame. A close-fitting shirt provides an outer depth constraint where its fabric is visible. Straps, shoulder plates and the hollow under a loose shirt require other evidence. Retain the shape between observations from the intact anatomical surface. Label a skin depth inferred beneath clothing as an estimate.

When fitting an arm, restrict section measurements and displacements to that limb. Blend the shoulder attachment through the deltoid and axillary fold. Compare the saved rib-cage points and the entire chest patch before and after the arm edit in the same rest pose. If the edit pulls a pectoral toward the elbow, reinstates a second chest peak, or produces a ledge beneath the ribs, go back to the intact surface and correct the region or correspondence. A matching arm length can coexist with those defects.

Verify the exported skin under the same light from the front, a three-quarter angle and profile. Raise each arm with the rib cage held still. Track the medial chest in rib-cage coordinates and inspect the transition through the moving axilla. For a rest-shape defect, correct the fit. For a motion-only defect, inspect shoulder and arm influences on the chest, rebind the transition and re-export. Repeat the original limb-length and garment tests on that GLB. Measure the same vertex IDs before and after, with separate samples over the medial pectoral area and the outer chest approaching the axilla. A sternum-only sample can stay still while lateral tissue stretches. Inspect a close-up of the transition at the same pose and camera angle. Compare posed and rest edge lengths across the anterior axillary fold. Inspect the worst edge as well as the distribution. On a narrowed fitted section, neighboring points can have very different upper-arm influences. Blend those weights over distance on the final skin and retest the garment over that fold.

## Chest, back and arms

1. Mark the neck base, shoulder joint, chest apex, lower rib cage and waist on the source and registered suit panels. Put marks in the calibrated working frame. Compare the *same anatomical station* across views. When one edit shifts the chest vertically, align that station to the source or redo the edit before using its profile depth. Look at a three-quarter view too. Two chest peaks on one mesh can come from applying measured depth at a different height from the template's chest peak.
2. On a template fit, recover the undistorted rib cage and shoulder surface from the matching prior. Map its landmarks to the measured heights in anatomical order. Fit width and depth together at those stations, interpolating smoothly between them. Blend across the clavicle, scapula and waist into the adjacent surface. Inspect horizontal sections for folds and rotate from the sternum to the nape to find ledges hidden in a frontal silhouette.
3. For a pinched upper arm, swollen forearm or kinked wrist, mark shoulder, elbow, wrist and palm on each side. Slice perpendicular to the local limb axis at the deltoid, biceps, elbow, forearm belly and wrist. Isolate the arm faces at the armpit so the cut excludes the rib cage. Compare width, front-to-back thickness and centre with the matching suit section. Trace the visible arm edge in profile even where it lies inside the torso silhouette. Map measurements by distance along each shoulder–elbow or elbow–wrist segment. A horizontal image row through a sloping arm gives an oblique cut. Where the torso occludes profile depth, use visible skin and the prior's cross-section as an estimate. Fit the native upper arm and forearm around their own axes, blend through the elbow, and carry the wrist section into the palm. Preserve finger spacing. Check the deltoid and armpit at rest and on arm raise, and watch the wrist through elbow flexion. If only motion introduces a crease, inspect the pivots and weights at that joint.

### Deltoid to biceps and the worn sleeve

When the shoulder dips into the biceps, place several closely spaced sections between the deltoid insertion and mid-biceps on each side. Give stations as a fraction of the shoulder-to-elbow distance. Compare the two front contour edges and the traced arm depth with cuts on the bare export. At a station where the arm merges with the torso, label the visible outer edge and the inferred inner edge separately. Slice the sleeve at the same station and pose. Record the bare diameter, dressed outer diameter and reference clothing profile. Repeat through the arm swing. Check the axillary skin for self-intersections as well as the garment against skin. A changing gap under the arm can come from two nearby surfaces following different bones.

For a profile mismatch, isolate torso faces from the arm, sword, straps and shoulder plate. In each horizontal row check the anterior and posterior target points separately, then check the height of the neck base, shoulder tip, shoulder blade and waist. A side silhouette can follow the arm or an accessory even when the body-level outline score is good. Use visible shirt or skin sections to constrain chest and back. Distinguish measured garment exterior from the estimated skin beneath it.
