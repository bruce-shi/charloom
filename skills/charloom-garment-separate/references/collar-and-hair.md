# Collar fragments and fused hair

English · [简体中文](collar-and-hair.zh-CN.md)

With the body hidden, turn the isolated head and locks over. Trace the garment edge on the original textured triangles from two directions. Check the back of each lock where fabric touches hair. Use attachment, depth and the atlas together to decide ownership. Record the reviewed faces against the source hash. Split a mixed patch at that edge, put its fabric faces in the garment and remove loose fabric islands from the bare stage. If fabric is painted onto retained skin or hair, repair those texels from adjacent observed material.

If removing fabric opens a lock, preserve its visible outer surface and reconstruct the missing inner surface from the lock's local cross-sections. Sample albedo from the same lock and transfer the adjacent hair weights. Inspect the isolated strand against a contrasting background, then restore the body and turn the head. Look for holes, pale slivers and detached triangles from behind the shoulder. Check the dressed stage for the intended collar and its clearance during the same turn.

Inspect the uncut head component from the front, side and back before assigning scalp faces to a separate hair layer. Short hair painted onto the only surface of the skull belongs with that retained head and its UVs. Separate actual locks, caps or additional hair shells by their geometry. Hide the hair and collar layers together and turn the head through a full rotation; the forehead, ears, crown and back of the skull should still form a head. For a painted collar, review the boundary at texture resolution before choosing geometry cuts or a local albedo repair.
